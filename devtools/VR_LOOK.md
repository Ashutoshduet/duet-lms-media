# What the best VR learning apps look like, and how DUET STEM matches it

Survey of Meta Quest learning apps (YouTube trailers and gameplay, Nov 2026), to set the bar for how real our content
must look. Reference the owner chose: **Human Anatomy VR (Virtual Medicine)**.

## Realistic tier (the bar)
| App | Subject | What makes it look real |
|---|---|---|
| Human Anatomy VR (Virtual Medicine) | Biology | Clean real-looking room (wood wall, ceiling light panels, window blinds, clock); PBR-textured organs, bones, vessels at real size; parts pulled out and floating in the air; translucent white hands that follow your fingers; frosted-glass info panels with leader lines and labels |
| VR Chemistry Lab (MIMBUS) | Chemistry | Digital twin of a real lab: white benches, real glassware, gloves box, taps; realistic hands |
| MEL Chemistry VR | Chemistry | Bright clean lab, then zoom down to atoms and ions (Na⁺, Cl⁻ lattice) |
| Blueplanet VR | Nature, geography | Photogrammetry scans of real places (Borobudur, coastlines); glass map panel to travel |
| National Geographic Explore VR | Nature, ecosystems | Photoreal landscapes (Machu Picchu, Antarctica); a camera to take photos of animals |
| theBlu, Ocean Rift | Ocean ecosystem | Realistic underwater light, whales, rays, shoals of fish |
| Nature Treks VR | Nature | Calm realistic beaches, forests and seas |
| InMind, Cellscape, The Body VR | Biology (cells, brain) | Glowing micro-world journeys inside the body and the cell |

## Stylised tier (fun, not real)
Math World VR (colourful game), Prisms VR (low-poly, real-world maths problems), HoloLAB Champions, Futuclass
(physics and chemistry escape rooms), Physics Playground, NeoTrie VR and Calcflow (maths tools).
No one does maths or physics in photo-real, real-size places: that is DUET's gap to fill.

## Rules for DUET content
1. Real size, real place: the student stands inside the situation (a field, a kitchen, a classroom, a train).
2. Real materials: PBR textures (wood, tile, metal, cloth, soil), soft shadows, an indoor environment light.
3. Real hands: translucent white hand models follow the student's fingers; the real Quest controller shows when
   controllers are used (lib/xr/, from WebXR Input Profiles, MIT).
4. Clear labels: numbers and names on the object, with short lines; glass panels for text.
5. Things react to what the student did (no fixed outcome).
6. Assets: only CC0 / CC-BY / public-domain sources, adapted to our scenes, with credits — Poly Haven, ambientCG,
   NASA, Smithsonian Open Access, NIH 3D, Wikimedia Commons, CC-BY Sketchfab models. Videos on YouTube are for
   ideas only; nothing is copied from them or from other apps.
