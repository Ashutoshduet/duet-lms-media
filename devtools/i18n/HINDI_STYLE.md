# How the DUET apps speak Hindi

The Hindi text is read aloud by the narrator and shown on screen. It must sound like a good teacher
talking to a class, not like a sentence translated word by word from English.

1. **Speak, don't translate.** First understand what the English means, then say it the way a Hindi-speaking
   teacher would say it in class. Change the order of ideas if Hindi needs it.
   - Not: "आपका दोस्त आपकी आँख के ठीक नीचे से खंभे के नीचे तक मेज़रिंग टेप खींचता है।"
   - But: "आपका दोस्त आपके पैरों के पास से खंभे तक फ़ीता खींचकर दूरी नापेगा।"
2. **Short sentences, one idea each.** Break long English sentences into two or three. Avoid "जो… वह…" chains.
3. **Talk to the student.** Use "आप" with polite commands: देखिए, गिनिए, रखिए, बताइए.
   Use "चलिए" to start something together ("चलिए, पास से भी जाँच लेते हैं।").
4. **Feel the moment.** At a discovery, react the way a teacher would, but not in every line:
   "देखा? बिल्कुल फ़िट बैठ गया!", "अरे! सिर्फ़ एक-तिहाई ही भरा।", "यही तो मज़े की बात है।"
   Use "!" and "?" honestly; they change how the narrator says the sentence.
5. **NCERT words, made easy.** Keep the textbook term the student must learn (परिमाप, उन्नयन कोण, तिर्यक रेखा,
   चतुर्थांश) and explain it once in everyday words: "परिमाप, यानी चारों ओर की पूरी लंबाई".
   Use the everyday word everywhere else (प्लस/माइनस next to धनात्मक/ऋणात्मक, फ़ीता for measuring tape).
6. **Grammar and gender.** Check every verb against its noun: अक्ष (m) — "x-अक्ष आड़ा जाता है";
   रस्सी (f) — "रस्सी चारों ओर गई"; गेंद (f) — "गेंद कितनी दूर जाएगी?". Use nukta where it is said:
   ज़मीन, फ़ीता, ज़्यादा, तरीक़ा.
7. **Numbers and units.** Write units as words in narrated text (15 मीटर, 30 सेंटीमीटर, 20 वर्ग मीटर,
   2.36 घन मीटर). Short on-screen labels may keep m, cm, m².
8. **Pauses.** A comma is a short breath for the narrator; "।" is a full stop. Put a comma where a teacher would pause.
9. **Questions sound like questions.** "बताइए, तीसरा कोण कितना होगा?" — not a statement with a question mark.
10. **Hinglish (hg)** follows the same rules in Roman script: how a teacher in a Hindi-belt city actually mixes
    the two languages, with English for technical words students already use (angle, radius, area).
