More boards: Nano, Mega 2560, ESP32 (batch 4)

The Nano, Mega 2560 and ESP32 DevKit V1 boards, the ESP32-WROOM-32 module, the LED bar graph, the resistor network, the touch plate, the bar magnet and its clamp stand, the phone and the WiFi router are modelled in code by DUET STEM from the parts' datasheet sizes, with their printed artwork drawn in code. They are generic boards of our own design, compatible with the Arduino Nano, the Arduino Mega 2560 and the ESP32 DevKit V1; no Arduino, Espressif or other brand logo is used. "Arduino", "ESP32" and "ESP32-WROOM-32" are named only to say what the boards are compatible with. The phone's browser, WiFi and Bluetooth are simulated inside the app: nothing is sent over a real network.

The micro:bit lessons (batch 5)

The micro:bit board, the crocodile clips, the plant pot with its soil probes, the servo gate, the compass card, the battery pack, the strap and the other props are modelled in code by DUET STEM from published sizes (the board: 51.6 × 42 × 1.6 mm), with their printed artwork drawn in code. The board is a BBC micro:bit V2 compatible board of our own design; no micro:bit logo or other brand artwork is used. "BBC micro:bit" is a trademark of the Micro:bit Educational Foundation and is named only to say what the lessons teach. The Python interpreter, the 5 × 5 display font, the blocks and the simulation of the board (display, buttons, accelerometer, compass, sensors, radio, pins) are written by DUET STEM. The patterns of the built-in images (Image.HEART, Image.HAPPY, …) and the names and notes of the short built-in sound effects (music.BA_DING, POWER_UP, …) follow MicroPython for the BBC micro:bit (Damien P. George and contributors, MIT licence), so that programs written for a real micro:bit show the same pictures; the longer tunes are our own transcriptions of old public-domain melodies. Chip part numbers (nRF52833, nRF52820, LSM303AGR, SG90) are named only to describe the parts.

Raspberry Pi: Pico and Pi 4 (batch 6)

The Raspberry Pi Pico (51 × 21 mm) and Raspberry Pi 4 Model B (85 × 56 mm) boards, the GPIO pinout card, the 5-inch HDMI screen with its terminal, the cables, the active buzzer and the Pi on the robot car are modelled in code by DUET STEM from the official mechanical drawings and datasheets (pin and connector positions, hole sizes), with their printed artwork drawn in code: our own work, no downloaded 3D model or texture. No Raspberry Pi logo or other brand artwork is used; "Raspberry Pi", "Pico" and "RP2040" are trademarks of Raspberry Pi Ltd and are named only to say what the lessons teach. Chip part numbers (RP2040, W25Q16JV, BCM2711, VL805, LPDDR4) are named only to describe the parts. The MicroPython and Python interpreter (py.js, shared with the micro:bit lessons), the machine, time, gpiozero, RPi.GPIO, board and adafruit_dht modules and the simulation of the boards are written by DUET STEM; they follow the published interfaces of MicroPython for the RP2040 (MIT licence), gpiozero (BSD licence) and Adafruit CircuitPython DHT (MIT licence), so that a program written here runs on a real board; none of their code is copied.

School-lab modules and block coding (batch 3)

The 16×2 LCD with its I2C backpack, the 0.96" OLED, the 7-segment display, the DHT11, the soil probe, the PIR sensor, the relay module, the IR receiver and its remote, the 4×4 keypad, the 28BYJ-48 stepper and its ULN2003 board, the RC522 reader with its card and key tag, the MQ-2 gas sensor, the pump, the battery box and the 230 V lamp and fan box are modelled in code by DUET STEM from the parts' datasheet sizes, with their printed artwork drawn in code. So are the rooms beside the bench (the window and the cup of chai, the tulsi pot and its water tank, the living room, the door and its latch, the locker, the kitchen with its gas cylinder, the pan-tilt head and its targets, the turntable); their wood is the Oak Veneer 01 texture above. The libraries' behaviour (LiquidCrystal_I2C, Adafruit SSD1306 / GFX text and shapes, DHT, IRremote, Keypad, Stepper, MFRC522) is simulated by our own code; the 5×7 letters of the LCD and OLED follow the classic HD44780 / glcdfont pattern (Adafruit GFX glcdfont.c, BSD licence). The block editor is our own (no Blockly code). Part numbers (PCF8574, SSD1306, HC-SR501, VS1838B, ULN2003, 28BYJ-48, RC522, MQ-2) are named only to describe the parts.
